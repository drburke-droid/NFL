## Read
**The optimal strategy is "take the best value, full stop."** Three strategies own the
podium — `neutral` (2.32), `elite_qb` (2.43), `cheap_qb` (2.87) — and together they win
**90%** of all leagues. The other nine finish 5.4–10.1 and essentially never win.

What separates the winners from the losers is NOT the QB approach — it's that the top
three are **neutral across RB/WR/TE** (they take the best available value), while every
loser **forces a skill-position tilt** and overpays its favored type. With everyone
holding the same good projections, distortion is pure loss.

- **QB approach barely matters.** Pay up for an elite QB, punt QB, or take QB value as
  it comes — all three finish top-3. (Neutral edges elite_qb edges cheap_qb, within noise.)
- **Forcing an RB archetype actively hurts** — which is exactly why the two hypothesized
  combos fail: `cheapQB + 2 workhorse RB` lands 6th (7.29) and `eliteQB + pass-catching RB`
  lands 11th (8.64). The cheap/elite-QB *part* is fine; it's the forced workhorse/receiving-RB
  preference that tanks them by passing up better value at other RBs/WRs.
- zero-RB (5.35) is the "least-bad" tilt; everything else (WR-heavy, TE-premium, hero-RB,
  robust-RB, stars-and-scrubs) is worse.

**Bottom line:** there is no clever positional or archetype draft strategy that beats
simply drafting the best projected value by VORP regardless of position. "Strategies"
like zero-RB or pass-catching-RB are heuristics for drafters *without* good projections;
once your projections are good, they only cost you. (Caveat: best-ball scoring, same
projections for all — the cleanest isolation of *roster construction*; the magnitude of
the gap is amplified because 9 of 12 owners handicap themselves, but the ranking is robust.)
