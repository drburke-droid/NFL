## Read
**The premise is half right.** On the things that mean "reliable points," QB wins clearly:
- **Availability:** QB plays the most games (13.8) and misses significant time least (26% of seasons <14 g) vs RB's 12.9 g / 40%. Your QB shows up.
- **Weekly steadiness:** QB has the lowest week-to-week volatility (CV 0.49 vs ~0.65 for RB/WR/TE) — fewer donut weeks.
- **Predictability:** QB projections are the most accurate (error 18% of mean vs 23–29%).

**But two facts cut against paying up for QB:**
- **QB is the *least* repeatable year-to-year** (YoY PPG corr 0.36 — lowest; TE 0.53 highest). Which QB finishes #1 vs #8 shuffles annually (TD variance, team context), so you can't reliably *target* the elite QB a year out.
- **QB has by far the smallest VORP spread (7.2).** The 12th QB is nearly as good as the 1st; RB studs sit ~13.7 above replacement. There's simply little QB VORP to buy.

**Net:** a unit of QB VORP is steadier and more bankable, but there's much less of it *and* you can't pick the right one in advance. The effects roughly cancel — exactly what the sims show: paying up for QB is ~neutral in best-ball and a tiny +0.3 places in set-lineup H2H (reliability protects your weekly floor), both within noise.

## Practical takeaway
- **Don't systematically pay up for QB.** VORP already prices the small spread correctly, and the reliability nudge is offset by QB's poor year-to-year repeatability.
- The edge is second-order: at the **same price**, prefer a steady elite QB over a boom/bust one, and it matters a touch more in H2H than best-ball.
- Reliability argues more strongly the *other* way — **fade injury-fragile, boom/bust, hard-to-project RBs** relative to raw VORP, which our fade-risk / archetype-aging / injury-prior tools already do.
