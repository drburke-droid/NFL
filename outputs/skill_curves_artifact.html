Per-play skill over a career: every QB, RB, WR and TE since 2011

One number per player-season that usage cannot inflate: a play-weighted composite of per-play efficiency (QB: EPA/dropback + CPOE · RB: EPA/rush + success rate · WR/TE: EPA/target + YAC over expected), z-scored against the same position in the same season. 0 = league average for the position that year; +1 = one standard deviation above. In the efficiency views, volume, touches and team role are absent by construction — what the player did with each play, not how many plays he was given. The default alpha view then adds earned volume back on purpose: commanding targets is itself skill, and the blend carries far more forward signal than efficiency alone. Four views: alpha (skill + volume) (default) — opponent-adjusted per-play efficiency blended with Kalman-smoothed earned volume, 50/50 at WR/TE and 70/30 at QB/RB, weights chosen out-of-time against next-season PPG; true skill — a Kalman-smoothed estimate of the raw per-play composite that treats every season as a noisy measurement, weighted by play count and the metric's reliability at the position; Elo — the same smoothing applied to opponent- and teammate-adjusted effects (ridge adjusted plus-minus: every player-game solved jointly with the defense faced, so a brutal schedule or a shadow corner no longer counts against the player); and observed (each raw season at face value). Christian McCaffrey is the motivating case: his workload and PPG projection hold, but his skill curve is in the first sustained decline of his career — and the Elo view confirms it wasn't schedule-driven.

McCaffrey against the RB position band, season by season

The solid line is his Kalman-smoothed true skill; the faint dots are each season's observed value (sized by qualifying plays). The smoothing makes the story sharper, not softer: his true skill held an almost perfectly flat +0.33z from 2017 through 2023 — the observed 2023 "+1.34z peak" was mostly a one-season spike on top of a stable edge — and 2024–25 is the first sustained decline of his career, down to +0.08z. Because 2025 came on 311 plays (his biggest workload since 2019), the estimator treats it as high-confidence evidence, not noise. The role survived; the per-play edge is going.

How to read it — and how much to trust it, by position

Year-to-year correlation says how much of the metric is persistent skill vs. one-season noise. Play-by-play detail 2011–2025 (nflverse); minimum plays per season: 90 dropbacks (QB), 48 designed carries (RB), 24 targets (WR/TE).

Method: per-play values from every regular-season snap, play-weighted per season, z-scored within position × season (era-independent), 65% primary + 35% secondary metric. Because the pool is re-centered every year, a flat line means "aging exactly as fast as the league improves."

True-skill smoothing: a Kalman filter + RTS smoother per career. Latent skill mean-reverts toward the position average (persistence φ) and drifts by Q per season; each observed season measures it with noise proportional to 1/plays. φ, Q and the noise scale are grid-searched per position to best predict the next observed season — the winning fit beats "repeat last season" by 17% (QB) to 23% (WR/TE). The optimizer chose heavy shrinkage for RB/WR/TE (measurement noise ≈ 2.5× signal for a median-plays season) and light shrinkage for QB — matching the stickiness table above. Full-workload seasons move the estimate most; a 50-play injury season barely moves it. Tables player_skill_true / player_skill_seasons, built by scripts/skill_kalman.py, scripts/fetch_skill_signals.py, scripts/skill_trajectory_study.py; findings in outputs/reports/skill_trajectories.md.

Elo view: ridge adjusted plus-minus per position × season (scripts/skill_elo.py → player_skill_elo): EPA/play per player-game solved jointly with a defense effect for the opponent faced — teammates in the same game share the defense term, and each defense is identified from every offense that played it. WR defense effects are split WR1 vs WR2/3, which rediscovers shadow corners from play-by-play alone (biggest WR1-specific suppression seasons: 2011 NYJ Revis, 2025 HOU Stingley, 2020 NO Lattimore, 2018 MIN Rhodes). Predicts next season's raw skill better than the unadjusted estimate at QB/WR/TE (e.g. WR r 0.25 vs 0.22); RB is a wash. Coverage ~90% of qualifying seasons — missing seasons show as gaps in the Elo view.

Alpha view: alpha = w·Elo + (1−w)·volume, both Kalman-smoothed careers; volume is plays per game, era-z-scored within position × season. w is chosen per position by partial correlation with next-season PPG controlling prior PPG and age (all causal, no future data): w=0.5 at WR/TE, 0.7 at QB/RB. The blend beats pure efficiency at forecasting next-season fantasy value at every position — WR partial r 0.10 → 0.25, TE 0.12 → 0.26. A passer-adjustment term was also tested and failed validation (the QB dummy is collinear with his own WR1 and steals star credit — WR r drops 0.25 → 0.22), so QB context is deliberately not modeled.
