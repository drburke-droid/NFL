Why this exists. Behind this page is an NFL projection model that already ranks with the best fantasy sites in the business — CBS, ESPN, FantasyPros, FantasySharks, FFToday, numberFire, FleaFlicker and NFL.com — and goes head-to-head with the DraftKings player-prop lines every game. The open question is whether people can make it better: one sharp fan, or the combined lean of many, might see things the numbers don't. Every arrow you place is scored against what really happened, and if your picks keep beating the model, they earn a seat in it.
How accuracy is measured, in plain words. Every projection is a guess at a player's fantasy points. After the game we take the difference between the guess and what he really scored, ignore whether it was too high or too low, and average those misses over every player who actually took the field. A 4.0 means the typical projection was about four fantasy points off. Lower is better. Players who were inactive don't count for or against anyone — we grade the model the hard way, on players who played.

The fine print
Every arrow is scored once the game's box score is in, against the projection the model actually shipped for that game. Direction = the real number moved the way the arrow said. Error removed = fantasy points closer (or farther) than the model, summed. Arrows placed after a game kicks off do not count. Shadow mode: nothing here changes the model — yet.
